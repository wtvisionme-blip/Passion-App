# A to Z Client Engagement Playbook
## AI Compliance Architect — Complete Client Journey

---

## OVERVIEW

Every client engagement follows 8 phases. Each phase has a clear goal, specific tasks, and deliverables. Your job is to guide the client through all 8 — some phases take days, some take months depending on the size of the organization.

```
PHASE 1 → First Contact & Qualification
PHASE 2 → Discovery Call
PHASE 3 → Proposal & Scoping
PHASE 4 → Contract & Kickoff
PHASE 5 → AI Audit & Assessment
PHASE 6 → Gap Analysis & Risk Report
PHASE 7 → Policy & Framework Build
PHASE 8 → Implementation, Training & Ongoing Support
```

---

## PHASE 1 — First Contact & Qualification

### Goal
Determine if this organization is a good fit before investing time in a full discovery call.

### How You Get Leads
- LinkedIn outreach to CHROs, CISOs, General Counsel, COOs
- Referrals from attorneys, accountants, IT consultants
- Speaking at industry events or webinars
- Content marketing (posts about AI risk and compliance)

### Qualification Questions (Pre-Call Screen)
Ask these via email or LinkedIn before booking a discovery call:
1. How many employees does your organization have?
2. What industry are you in?
3. Are your teams currently using any AI tools?
4. Have you received any regulatory pressure around AI yet?
5. What prompted you to reach out now?

### Green Flags (Good Fit)
- 50+ employees
- Regulated industry (healthcare, finance, legal, insurance, government)
- Already using AI tools with no governance
- Recent audit, incident, or regulatory inquiry
- Leadership is aware AI is a risk

### Red Flags (Poor Fit — Proceed Carefully)
- Fewer than 10 employees with no AI usage
- No budget allocated for compliance
- Leadership doesn't believe AI compliance is necessary

### Your Output from Phase 1
- Qualified lead confirmed
- Discovery call scheduled
- Basic research done on their industry's regulatory requirements

## PHASE 2 — Discovery Call

### Goal
Understand their current state, pain points, and what they need. Build trust. Leave them feeling like you already understand their problem better than they do.

### Duration
45 to 60 minutes

### Your Role
You are NOT selling yet. You are listening, diagnosing, and showing expertise through the quality of your questions.

### Call Structure
**Minutes 0–5: Rapport & Agenda Setting**
- Thank them for their time
- Set the agenda: "I'd like to spend most of this call understanding where you are today with AI. At the end I'll share what I'm seeing across similar organizations and what typically helps."

**Minutes 5–35: Discovery Questions**
Run through the 7 sections from the Discovery Call Framework (see template 07):
1. Their AI footprint
2. Data and privacy practices
3. Regulatory environment
4. Past risk and incidents
5. Current policies and governance
6. People and culture
7. Budget and decision-making

**Minutes 35–50: Diagnosis & Reflection**
Summarize what you heard back to them. This is powerful — it shows you listened and builds trust.

Example: "What I'm hearing is you have about 200 employees, at least 6 AI tools in active use, no formal AI policy, and an audit coming up in Q3. That's a high-risk window. Here's what I typically see happen in that situation..."

**Minutes 50–60: Next Steps**
- Tell them you'll send a proposal within 3–5 business days
- Ask: "Is there anyone else who should be part of this conversation going forward?"
- Confirm timeline urgency: "Is Q3 a hard deadline for you?"

### Your Output from Phase 2
- Discovery call notes documenting
  - All AI tools mentioned
  - Regulatory frameworks that apply
  - Biggest risks identified
  - Decision maker and budget holder confirmed
  - Timeline and urgency level

## PHASE 3 — Proposal & Scoping

### Goal
Deliver a professional proposal that clearly describes what you will do, what they will receive, and what it costs.

### Proposal Structure

**1. Executive Summary (1 paragraph)**
Restate their situation in their own words. Show you understood what they told you.

**2. Scope of Work**
List exactly what you will deliver. Be specific — vague scope leads to scope creep.

Common deliverables:
- AI Systems Inventory (list of every AI tool in use)
- Risk Assessment Report
- Gap Analysis against applicable regulations
- AI Use Policy (written for their org)
- Vendor AI Contract Addendum Template
- Employee AI Training Program
- AI Governance Framework document
- Ongoing monthly compliance monitoring (retainer)

**3. Timeline**
Phase-by-phase with estimated weeks for each.

**4. Investment**
Your pricing. Common models:
- One-time assessment: $5,000 – $25,000 depending on org size
- Project-based (full framework build): $15,000 – $75,000
- Monthly retainer (ongoing): $2,000 – $10,000/month

**5. About You**
2–3 sentences on your background and why you are qualified.

**6. Next Steps**
"Sign and return the attached agreement to get started."

### Your Output from Phase 3
- Proposal document sent within 5 business days of discovery call
- Follow-up email scheduled for 3 days after sending

## PHASE 4 — Contract & Kickoff

### Goal
Get the agreement signed, collect the first payment, and run a kickoff meeting to align on the engagement.

### Contract Must Include
- Scope of work (exactly what you will deliver)
- Timeline and milestones
- Payment schedule (50% upfront is standard)
- Confidentiality / NDA clause
- Data handling agreement (you will see sensitive data)
- Change order process (how you handle scope changes)
- Termination clause

### Kickoff Meeting Agenda (60 minutes)
1. Introduce yourself and your process
2. Confirm the scope and deliverables
3. Introduce your data collection process
4. Schedule interviews or workshops with key stakeholders
5. Set communication cadence (weekly check-in recommended)
6. Assign a point of contact on their side

### Your Output from Phase 4
- Signed contract
- First payment received
- Kickoff meeting completed
- Project plan shared with client
- Stakeholder interview schedule set

## PHASE 5 — AI Audit & Assessment

### Goal
Build a complete picture of the organization's current AI usage, data flows, policies, and risk exposure.

### What You Do

**Step 1: AI Systems Inventory**
Find every AI tool the organization uses — approved and unapproved.
- Send a survey to department heads
- Interview IT, HR, Legal, Operations, Finance, Marketing
- Review software procurement records
- Check browser extensions and SaaS subscriptions
- Ask about personal devices (BYOD)

For each tool document:
- Tool name and vendor
- Department using it
- Data types flowing into it
- Whether a vendor agreement covers AI data use
- Whether employees have had any training on it

**Step 2: Data Flow Mapping**
Trace where sensitive data goes when it enters an AI system.
- Does the vendor use your data to train their models?
- Is data stored outside your country?
- Is PII, PHI, or financial data involved?
- Are third-party subprocessors involved?

**Step 3: Policy Review**
Review all existing policies for AI-related gaps:
- Acceptable use policy
- Data classification policy
- Vendor management policy
- Privacy policy
- Employee handbook

**Step 4: Regulatory Mapping**
Confirm which regulations apply and what they require:
- HIPAA (healthcare): AI must not expose PHI without safeguards
- GDPR/CCPA (privacy): AI decisions involving personal data need disclosure
- SOC 2 (security): AI vendor access must be controlled and audited
- EU AI Act: High-risk AI systems require conformity assessments
- Financial regulations: Explainability of AI-driven decisions required

**Step 5: Stakeholder Interviews**
Interview 5–10 people across the org:
- CISO or IT Director
- Legal/Compliance Officer
- HR Director
- Department heads who use AI most
- A few frontline employees

Ask each: What AI tools do you use? What do you put into them? Do you know what happens to that data?

### Your Output from Phase 5
- Complete AI Systems Inventory spreadsheet
- Data Flow Map document or diagram
- Policy Gap list
- Regulatory Applicability Matrix
- Interview notes and findings summary

## PHASE 6 — Gap Analysis & Risk Report

### Goal
Tell the client exactly where they stand, what their risks are, and rank those risks by severity.

### Gap Analysis Structure

For each regulatory framework that applies, document:
- What the regulation requires
- What the organization currently does
- The gap between the two
- Risk level: Critical / High / Medium / Low
- Recommended action

### Risk Report Structure

**Executive Summary**
2–3 paragraphs for leadership. Non-technical. Focus on business risk, not technical detail.

**Critical Findings**
List the top 3–5 most urgent risks. These need immediate action.

**Detailed Findings**
Full breakdown of every gap found, organized by category:
- Data Privacy Risks
- Governance Risks
- Vendor/Contract Risks
- Employee Behavior Risks
- Regulatory Compliance Risks

**Risk Heatmap**
A visual grid showing likelihood vs. impact for each risk.

**Recommended Remediation Roadmap**
Prioritized list of actions with suggested owners and timelines.

### How to Present It
Always present the risk report in a live meeting — never just email it.
Walk them through the findings. Let them react. This is where clients feel the value of what you do.

### Your Output from Phase 6
- Gap Analysis spreadsheet
- Risk Report document (executive version and full version)
- Risk Heatmap
- Remediation Roadmap

## PHASE 7 — Policy & Framework Build

### Goal
Give the organization the actual tools, documents, and structure they need to operate compliantly.

### Core Deliverables You Build

**1. AI Use Policy**
Written in plain language. Covers:
- What AI tools employees are allowed to use
- What data they cannot put into AI systems
- How to request approval for a new AI tool
- Consequences of violating the policy

**2. AI Governance Framework**
The organizational structure for managing AI compliance ongoing:
- Who owns AI governance (AI Committee or designated role)
- How new AI tools are evaluated and approved
- How incidents are reported and handled
- Review schedule (quarterly or annual AI audit)

**3. Vendor AI Addendum**
A contract clause to add to vendor agreements requiring:
- Disclosure of how your data is used in their AI systems
- No training on your data without explicit consent
- Data deletion rights
- Security and breach notification requirements

**4. AI Risk Register**
A living document tracking all known AI risks:
- Risk description
- Likelihood and impact
- Current controls
- Assigned owner
- Status

**5. Employee AI Training Program**
A training module covering:
- What AI tools are approved and how to use them
- What never to put into an AI system
- How to report an AI-related concern
- Basic AI literacy (how AI works, what hallucinations are)

### Your Output from Phase 7
- AI Use Policy (Word/PDF)
- AI Governance Framework document
- Vendor AI Addendum template
- AI Risk Register spreadsheet
- Employee Training deck or module

## PHASE 8 — Implementation, Training & Ongoing Support

### Goal
Help the client actually adopt what you built — and keep them compliant over time.

### Implementation Support
- Present the AI Use Policy to leadership for approval
- Facilitate employee training sessions (live or recorded)
- Help IT implement technical controls (tool blocking, approved tool list)
- Assist legal in updating vendor contracts with the AI addendum

### Ongoing Retainer (Monthly)
If the client signs on for ongoing support, your monthly work includes:
- Monitor regulatory changes that affect their industry
- Review any new AI tools being considered for adoption
- Update the AI Risk Register with new findings
- Conduct quarterly mini-audits
- Be available for questions and incident response
- Deliver a monthly compliance status report

### Annual Review
Once per year, conduct a full re-assessment:
- Has their AI footprint changed?
- Have regulations changed?
- Are policies still current and enforced?
- Are employees still trained?

### Your Output from Phase 8
- Training completion records
- Updated policies (if changes needed post-implementation)
- Monthly compliance status reports (retainer clients)
- Annual re-assessment report
